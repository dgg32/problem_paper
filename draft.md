Papers at Risk: Retraction Prognosis for Academic Publications
==============
***Using AI and a knowledge graph of the published literature to flag risky publications early***

Science moves forward by standing on the shoulders of past work, with every published paper serving as a building block for future discovery. When a paper contains flawed data, invalid conclusions, or outright fraud, it creates a compromised Jenga block in that structure. If the block is not swiftly identified and retracted, the flaw pollutes downstream science as more research builds upon it. Studies of retraction timing put the median delay at close to two years, and throughout that window, unsuspecting researchers keep citing the flawed work, letting it propagate deep into the literature.

The scientific community is not entirely defenseless against this threat, but its safeguards are stationed at extreme ends of the process, and neither can scale to match the volume and speed of retraction-worthy papers. At one end is the Retraction Watch database, which now logs more than 66,000 retractions and has become the field’s system of record. It is indispensable, but it is downstream by construction: a ledger of verdicts already delivered, reporting conclusions rather than suspicions, so by the time a paper appears in it, the harm is already done. At the other end are independent sleuths, exemplified by figures like Geng Hongwei, known online by his handle “耿同学” (“Classmate Geng”), who left his PhD program in 2025 to become a full-time research-integrity whistleblower. Through public social media channels, he draws high-profile attention to data anomalies, prompting universities and journals to open formal investigations into suspect publications. But sleuths like Geng face two fundamental limitations of their own: manual review cannot keep pace with the sheer volume of modern publishing, and whistleblowers often pay a steep personal and professional price for stepping forward, making the approach fundamentally unsustainable. What is missing is an automated, scalable early-warning system, one that watches the literature at the pace it is actually produced and raises the alarm before the harm is done.

This project, Papers at Risk, is built to close that gap. It first constructs a Neo4j knowledge graph seeded from Retraction Watch, then expands it to cover unretracted work using OpenAlex and PubMed. It then runs a series of targeted sensors across the graph, checking indicators like an author’s retraction history, journal integrity metrics, tell-tale signs of unvetted LLM generation, and numbers that are statistically impossible. Rather than rendering automated verdicts, these sensors combine their evidence into a ranked queue of high-risk papers, giving AI analytical pipelines and human sleuths a clear set of leads to investigate long before a formal retraction notice ever appears.
